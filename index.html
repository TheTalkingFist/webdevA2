What is Pixel Art?

According to Wikipedia, "Pixel art is a form of digital art drawn with graphical software where images are built using pixels as the only building block."

So, Pixel Art is essentially a form of art that uses relatively large squares (or pixels) to form an image.

In the 80s, all the way through the early 90s, virtually all 2D games used pixel art graphics due to the limitations of technology.

Up till now, however, Pixel Art has remained a distinct artstyle, both in video games and digital art.

Shading Techniques

In real life, shadows can be very smooth, with very long gradients. Gradients that don't really play well with the harshness of pixel edges.

Which means that pixel artists have to make do with whatever they can to make shadows look as good as possible. Luckily, we have quite a few shading techniques at our disposal.

Sprite Art

Sprites, in the context of pixel art, refers to a depiction of an object or person. Multiple sprites depicting multiple states of one object can be connected together in a spritesheet.

This is a well-known concept in game development, and essentially a must-know if you want to make a 2D game. However, sprites are universal and can be applied anywhere, including animations!

Sprites aren't limited to a 2D world, either! Early classics like DOOM use a technique called "Billboarding", placing 2D sprites in a 3D world and turning them to always face the player.
